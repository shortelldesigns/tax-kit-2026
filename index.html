Withholding or quarterly payments? How a W-2 + 1099 household can pay as it goes

Federal income tax is pay-as-you-go: you pay during the year through withholding, estimated payments, or both (IRS Topic No. 306). With a day job and a side business, you have more than one way to cover the side income. Here are the options the IRS describes:

Ways to cover tax on 1099 income when you also have W-2 wages
OptionHow it worksWhat the IRS says
More withholding at the day jobFile a new Form W-4 with your employer and enter an extra amount to withholdYou may be able to avoid estimated payments on other income this way. Form W-4 has a line for the extra amount (Form 1040-ES 2026; Estimated taxes)
Quarterly Form 1040-ES paymentsPay through Direct Pay, EFTPS, your Online Account, card, or check with a voucherFour 2026 due dates. You can also pay the full amount by April 15, 2026 (Form 1040-ES 2026)
Apply last year’s overpaymentCredit part or all of a 2025 refund to 2026 estimated taxListed as a payment option in Pub. 505 (2026)
A mix of the aboveWithholding covers part, estimates cover the restEstimated payments aren’t required if withholding in each payment period is at least one-fourth of your required annual payment, or if you’ll owe under $1,000 with your return (Pub. 505 (2026), “Estimated Tax Payments Not Required”)

The IRS Tax Withholding Estimator can help you decide whether to change your W-4. The workbook’s quarterly tab is a place to write down your own reserve estimate next to these choices. It doesn’t make the choice for you.

Self-employment tax: when it starts and one deduction people miss

Per the IRS, you must pay self-employment tax and file Schedule SE if your net earnings from self-employment were $400 or more. (Church employee income has its own, separate threshold.) Net earnings from a sole proprietorship or independent contracting are generally subject to SE tax (IRS — Self-employment tax).

The same IRS page notes that you can deduct the employer-equivalent portion of your SE tax when you figure adjusted gross income. W-2 employees can’t deduct the Social Security and Medicare taxes withheld from their wages. When you estimate a side-hustle reserve, use the Form 1040-ES worksheet, which includes this deduction. Don’t just apply one flat percentage to profit (Form 1040-ES 2026 worksheet; Pub. 505 (2026), Worksheet 2-3).

Year-end records checklist for a W-2 + 1099 side hustle

Gather these before you fill in any worksheet, whether it’s ours, a tax program’s, or a preparer’s. The IRS notes that business purchases and sales generate supporting documents, and that you must keep records as long as needed to prove the income or deductions on a return (IRS — Recordkeeping).

  • Every Form W-2 for 2026, plus your last pay stub for a year-to-date check of wages and withholding
  • Each 1099 you receive (for example 1099-NEC, 1099-K, or 1099-MISC), along with your own income log. Not every payment arrives with a form
  • Receipts and invoices for business expenses, grouped the way Schedule C groups them (About Schedule C)
  • A mileage log or trip sheets with a written business purpose. Pub. 463 counts a log kept weekly as a timely record (Pub. 463)
  • Square footage of the space used regularly and exclusively for business. The simplified option uses that area, up to 300 sq ft, and the regular method uses the percentage of your home (IRS — Simplified home office option)
  • Date, amount, and confirmation number for every 2026 estimated payment, including any 2025 overpayment you applied
  • Your 2025 return. Form 1040-ES uses it as the starting point for 2026 estimates and for the prior-year safe harbor (Form 1040-ES 2026)

How long to keep them: the IRS says 3 years in many cases, with longer periods in specific situations. For example, it’s 6 years if you leave out income worth more than 25% of the gross income shown on your return (IRS — How long should I keep records?).

What is the 2026 OASDI wage base in this kit?

$184,500, from the SSA contribution and benefit base and Form 1040-ES (2026). Self-employment Social Security tax (12.4%) shares that annual base with your day-job Box 3 Social Security wages. Remaining base (not below zero) is the maximum side-hustle earnings still subject to the 12.4% SE Social Security tax. Medicare (2.9%) has no wage-base cap.

Can I raise my W-4 withholding instead of making quarterly payments?

Often, yes. Form 1040-ES (2026) says wage earners may be able to avoid estimated payments on other income by filing a new Form W-4 to have more tax withheld. The IRS Tax Withholding Estimator can help you choose an amount (Form 1040-ES 2026; Tax Withholding Estimator).

My side income started in the fall. Did I miss the deadlines?

Not necessarily. Pub. 505 (2026) Table 2-1 says that if you first have income subject to estimated tax after August 31, your first payment is due January 15 of the next year. See the 2026 estimated tax dates guide for the full table (Pub. 505 (2026)).

Does paying the safe-harbor amount mean I won’t owe in April?

No. The IRS says you may still owe tax when you file even if you pay the required annual payment. The safe harbor is about the underpayment penalty, not your final balance (Form 1040-ES 2026). Not tax advice. Consult a tax professional or IRS resources for your situation.
